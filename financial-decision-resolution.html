Addendum, 4 September 2026: confirmed by the founder

The following are now Approved, superseding the rows below wherever they disagree:

  • Rider model (C, K.1): salaried, confirmed. Riders pay no subscription to Deligro and receive no per-order delivery commission.
  • EV bikes: Deligro provides the rider's EV bike and bears its running costs (maintenance, charging, other operating support) as a Deligro cost, never rider-funded. Bike allocation itself stays an offline register by design; only the rupee cost is tracked, in operational_expenses.
  • Tip (B, section 5 of the founder's brief): 100% to rider, confirmed. Never Deligro revenue.
  • COD custody chain (G): corrected, not the process this document originally proposed. The confirmed flow is Customer → Rider → Manager → Owner, not rider-to-Deligro directly. Each leg (collection, rider-to-manager, manager-to-owner) is now digitally recorded (deliveries.cod_collected_amount/cod_collected_at, and the new cod_handovers table), while the physical cash movement stays offline.
  • Offline execution principle, new: an offline process (payroll, bike maintenance paid in cash, a manager handing cash to the owner) is acceptable indefinitely for a single tier-3 city, but it must never mean an untracked financial transaction. Every financially material offline event gets a corresponding digital record; nothing about the physical movement itself is forced online.

Still unresolved and unchanged by this addendum: the rider's actual monthly salary, headcount and working hours; the commission rate and structure; the actual gateway fee; dual-approval thresholds; and the remaining items in K. See docs/DATABASE.md and migration 0045_cod_handovers_and_expenses.sql for what was built against the confirmed items above.

G

COD — the one official process

Approved, 4 September 2026 Superseded below: the confirmed process is Customer → Rider → Manager → Owner, not rider-to-Deligro directly. See the addendum at the top of this document.

The process this section originally proposed (rider remits straight to Deligro) was not the one the founder confirmed. Kept below for its reasoning on cash custody, since that part still holds: the rider is cash custodian, and remits onward, never to the vendor directly. The vendor is still paid by bank transfer at settlement regardless of how the customer paid.

StepRule
1. CollectionCustomer pays cash to the rider at delivery.
2. CustodyThe rider holds all COD cash collected that shift — a real, tracked liability the rider carries.
3. ConfirmationThe rider confirms each COD collection in-app immediately after collecting it — a required step, currently missing entirely.
4. Remittance confirmed flowThe rider hands the shift's accumulated cash to the manager; the manager hands the day's cash to the owner. Both legs are recorded (cod_handovers), receiving a digital record (amount, date, who handed it, who recorded it). The physical handover itself can happen offline.
5. ReconciliationShown side by side on /admin/cash-ledger: cash collected today against cash handed to the manager against cash handed to the owner. Not enforced or blocked, an operator's own judgement call, same as every other manual step here.
6. ShortfallA COD discrepancy event is raised against that rider's record. Resolution is gated by the same cautious HR policy as any pay-affecting incident — no automatic deduction.
7. OverpaymentAlso raised as a discrepancy, never silently absorbed as a "gain" — investigated the same way as a shortfall.
8. Cancelled orderNo cash was ever collected — no financial impact, excluded entirely (Refund case 1 in H).
9. RefundBecause Deligro now holds the cash within one day, a COD refund works exactly like an online refund — wallet credit to the customer, and a settlement correction against the vendor only if the vendor was at fault.

Why this process over rider-to-vendor handover: a rider serves many vendors across a shift and can't be relied on to revisit the same one promptly; centralizing cash at Deligro (realistic for a single-city operation) keeps the vendor payable formula identical for COD and online orders, and removes the "who actually holds it" ambiguity that today's settlement math gets wrong.
