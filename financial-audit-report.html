A

Current architecture

Deligro is a five-party marketplace sitting on Supabase/Postgres, with Razorpay wired in as the payment gateway but switched off by default. What follows is what exists, party by party, then table by table, then traced to its actual source of truth.

A.1The five parties, and the money each one touches

PartyPaysReceivesWhenWho bears the cost when it goes wrong
Customer Food subtotal + delivery fee + tax + optional tip, minus any coupon discount The order; a refund if cancelled/declined and it was prepaid At checkout (COD: nothing changes hands yet; online: immediately via Razorpay) Waits on a manual admin decision for any refund — no SLA in code
Vendor / Restaurant Platform commission + GST‑on‑commission + a flat per‑order charge, deducted from what they're owed Food value only (not delivery fee, not tax, not tip) — remitted by manual bank transfer, recorded after the fact Only when an admin builds and marks a settlement paid — no cron, no fixed date enforced by the system Fully exposed: nothing claws back a paid settlement if a refund lands afterward not found
Rider / Driver Nothing in-app not found Nothing in-app — compensation is a monthly salary paid entirely outside this system Off-platform, on whatever cadence payroll runs Entirely un-costed inside Deligro — no order can show what its own delivery actually cost
Deligro (platform) Razorpay's own transaction fee assumption — never deducted anywhere in code; any platform‑funded discount; approved refunds on prepaid orders Commission + GST‑on‑commission + other charges, per settled order Commission is only realised once a settlement is built — not at order time Carries every unreconciled gap: refund/settlement mismatches, uncosted rider spend, ungated commission-rate changes
Razorpay (gateway) — Its own processing fee on every online payment At capture Not Deligro's exposure directly, but the fee is invisible in every internal number

A phone-order operator (manager role) and an admin role also exist — both operational, neither a separate money party. The manager can place an order on a customer's behalf; only admin can move money-adjacent state.

B

Current money flow

Customer payment is not Deligro revenue. Only the commission slice is. Everything else in the customer's payment is either passed straight through (tax), held on the platform's behalf until settlement (food value), or has no defined destination in code at all (delivery fee, tip). Trace it stage by stage:

Stage 1
Customer
PaysFood + fee + tax + tip − discount
MethodCOD or Razorpay
Stage 2
Razorpay
HoldsFull order total
TakesGateway fee — uncosted
Stage 3
Deligro
KeepsCommission + GST + other charges
Owes vendorFood value, at settlement
Stage 4
Vendor
ReceivesFood value − commission − GST − charges
ViaManual bank transfer, recorded not moved
Stage 5
Rider
ReceivesNothing per-order
InsteadFixed monthly salary, off-platform

For a COD order, stages 1–2 don't happen — the vendor or rider collects cash directly at the door, and the code has no record that the cash actually changed hands (see C).

Do not treat "commission collected" as profit

The system's only visible platform revenue figure is the commission line in a settlement. It has never had Razorpay's own fee, rider salary cost, or refund exposure subtracted from it anywhere in the code. Reported as-is, platform revenue is structurally overstated relative to true contribution margin — not because of a bug, but because the ledger that would net those costs out was never built.

Flagging the ambiguity, not inventing a rule

The business premise ("riders are on a monthly subscription") and the code's own comments ("riders are salaried monthly") describe opposite directions of money — a subscription means the rider pays Deligro for access to jobs; a salary means Deligro pays the rider a wage. The codebase unambiguously implements neither as a live feature, but its own internal language assumes the salary direction. This needs the founder's own words, not a guess — see F.3. It is also not written in any of the project's internal .md docs; if a subscription model was ever specified, it most likely exists only in Deligro Feature Proposal.docx, Deligro Build Plan.docx, or Deligro-Quotation.pdf under Resources/srcdocs/ — binary files this audit could not read.

C.2Other gaps a scaling business will hit

Missing pieceWhy it matters
Payment gateway feesRazorpay's own cut is never deducted in any order, settlement, or report — every "revenue" figure the app shows is gross, not net, of what the gateway actually keeps.
COD cash-collection confirmationNothing on the deliveries table records that a rider actually collected cash at the door. For a cash order, the settlement math simply assumes the vendor already holds the money — there is no proof step, and no way to catch a rider who under-reports.
Rider cost allocationBecause riders are salaried entirely off-platform, no order — and no settlement — carries a rider cost line. True per-order contribution margin cannot be computed from this system today, for any order.
Payout executionSettlement "paid" means an admin typed a bank reference by hand. There is no RazorpayX/Route integration, no bank API — money physically moves entirely outside the application, and the app's record of it is only as trustworthy as that one manual entry.
Refund ↔ settlement reconciliationDetailed in D — a refund approved after its order has already been paid out is only flagged, never automatically corrected.
Negative vendor balancesThe settlement schema explicitly allows net_payable to go negative (vendor owes Deligro), but no code path collects it — there is no vendor-side debit, wallet, or invoice mechanism to actually recover that money.
Tax / GST invoicing & remittanceTax is collected into orders.tax_amount but nothing generates an invoice, tracks what's owed to the government, or marks it remitted.
Generic financial audit logSettlement and refund actions record who acted (paid_by, decided_by) but there is no unified, append-only log of every financial mutation with a before/after value — the kind an accountant or an investor's diligence team would expect.
Maker-checker / dual controlA single admin can build a settlement, mark it paid, and void it — with no second approval, at any amount. There is no threshold above which a second person must sign off.
Bank/gateway reconciliation jobNothing periodically pulls Razorpay's own settlement report and matches it against the internal payments table — the webhook is trusted once, and never checked again.
D

Financial risks

Ranked by severity. Items marked prior audit were already diagnosed with exact file/line citations in this repo's own AUDIT_REPORT_3.md (24 Aug 2026) and are still open as of this review — they don't need rediscovering, they need fixing. Items marked this audit are new synthesis from the financial-architecture pass.

SeverityRiskConcrete failure scenario
Critical A cancelled order can still be paid out to the vendor
The rider-completion write moves an order straight to delivered with no check on its current status, and most cancel paths never unwind the matching delivery row. Settlement and vendor-earnings queries select purely on status = 'delivered', so a cancelled-and-refunded order can still be swept into the next payout.
Prior audit — AUDIT_REPORT_3.md, driver-orders.ts / admin-settlements.ts / vendor-earnings.ts
Critical A refund approved after its settlement is paid is never clawed back
Order settles and is paid out Monday; the customer's refund is approved Wednesday. Deligro is now out the refund and has already paid the vendor for it, and nothing in the system flags the loss — the void function explicitly refuses to reverse a paid settlement and no correction mechanism exists.
Prior audit — AUDIT_REPORT_3.md, admin-settlements.ts (voidSettlement)
High Customer self-cancel has no optimistic lock
The cancel endpoint reads a status, checks it, then writes — with no WHERE status = <expected> guard, unlike every other status-writing path in the codebase. A vendor advancing the order in that same window gets silently overwritten, and the rider assignment is never unwound either.
Prior audit — AUDIT_REPORT_3.md, orders/[id]/cancel/route.ts
High Rider acceptance never re-validates order status server-side
acceptDelivery only relies on the UI hiding the Accept button — the server never confirms orders.status = 'ready' before creating a delivery row, a second route into the same resurrected-order money bug above.
Prior audit — AUDIT_REPORT_3.md, driver-orders.ts
High Delivery completion silently discards write errors
advanceDelivery drops the {error} from both its database writes and unconditionally reports success. If the order-status write fails after the delivery-status write succeeds, the rider is told the job is done while the customer's tracker, the vendor board and the admin board all stay stuck — and that order's money never enters settlement at all.
Prior audit — AUDIT_REPORT_3.md, driver-orders.ts
High No true cost or profit figure exists anywhere
Every "revenue" number in the app is gross commission — never netted against the Razorpay gateway fee, the rider's real off-platform cost, or open refund exposure. A founder reading the settlement totals as profit will consistently overestimate margin.

High COD cash has no collection record
A rider marks a cash order delivered with no field anywhere confirming cash was actually collected. The settlement math assumes the vendor already holds it. A rider or vendor under-reporting collected cash is undetectable from within the app.
This audit
Medium Draft settlements don't reflect refunds approved after the draft was built
A refund approved while an order is already sitting in a not-yet-paid draft settlement is silently absent from that draft's totals, with no on-screen indicator during refund approval that the order is already drafted.
Prior audit — AUDIT_REPORT_3.md
Medium Razorpay refunds carry no idempotency key
A retried refund call after a transient failure can trigger a second real refund at the gateway — the code deliberately surfaces the failure to a human instead of auto-retrying, but a human retrying by hand carries the same exposure.
This audit — src/lib/payments/refunds.ts
Medium Commission rate can drift between order placement and settlement
Commission is computed from whatever rate is live the moment an admin builds a settlement — not the rate in effect when the order was placed. If an admin changes the rate mid-month, orders placed under the old rate are still charged the new one at settlement. Not a bug — but there's no documented policy on which is intended.
This audit — src/lib/settlements/math.ts
Medium Pre-coupon-funding orders may still misattribute discount cost to vendors
Before the platform/vendor-funded coupon split existed, every discount silently came out of the vendor's payout. The fix (migration 0041) does not rewrite history automatically — a corrective backfill query exists in the migration's own comments but is left "for an operator to run or not." If it was never run, historical vendor payouts remain understated.
This audit — 0041_vendor_coupons.sql
Medium Settlement "paid" status is entirely trust-based
A single admin types a payment reference and flips a status; nothing verifies it against an actual bank transaction. There is no dual-control threshold, so a single compromised or careless admin account can misrecord — or fabricate — a payout at any size.

A client bypassing the app's own checkout flow and posting straight to the database API can submit zero for delivery_fee/tax_amount at insert time — total itself can't be forged, but these two feed straight into it. Already accepted as an open, documented risk by the project's own security audit.

E

Recommended architecture

The settlement engine already built is genuinely good — one formula, snapshotted rates, IST-correct periods. The gap is not arithmetic, it's that nothing closes the loop after money is recorded as moved. Five additions, in order of how directly they touch trust:

ComponentWhat it adds over today
Financial event logOne append-only table: every order-total change, coupon application, commission snapshot, refund decision, and settlement state change writes a row — actor, before value, after value, timestamp. Not a replacement for the existing tables, a spine underneath all of them.
Refund → settlement correctionWhen a refund is approved against an order that's already in a paid settlement, generate a correction line automatically — net it against the vendor's next settlement rather than leaving it as a silent, human-only note.
Rider cost ledgerEven before any per-order rider model exists, record a simple monthly-salary ÷ orders-delivered allocation per rider so contribution margin becomes computable. This does not require changing how riders are actually paid.
Platform revenue view (net, not gross)A single computed view: commission + GST-on-commission + other charges − gateway fees − platform-funded discounts − refund exposure − allocated rider cost. This is the number a CEO should actually be shown, not the raw commission line.
Gateway & bank reconciliation jobPull Razorpay's own settlement report and match it line-by-line against the internal payments table; separately, allow a bank-statement import that gets matched against vendor_settlements.payment_ref. Anything unmatched surfaces as an exception, not a silent gap.
Role-based finance permissionsToday it's binary — admin or not. Split "can view financial reports" from "can build a settlement" from "can mark one paid," and add a value threshold above which a second admin must approve.

None of this requires re-architecting what's there. vendor_settlements, vendor_settlement_orders, refunds, and payments stay exactly as they are — they become inputs to the event log and the reconciliation job, not replacements.

F

Questions for the founder

Only what genuinely cannot be answered by reading the project — the code is silent or contradictory on each of these.

1
What commission rate is actually live today, per vendor?

The schema's own default is 0%. If a real rate is configured, it lives only in the production database's platform_settings/restaurants rows — not in any file this audit could read.

2
Should a vendor be charged the commission rate live at order time, or the rate live at settlement time?

The system currently does the latter by construction. If that's not the intended policy, it needs an explicit decision before it's "fixed."

3
Which direction does rider money actually flow — does the rider pay Deligro, or does Deligro pay the rider?

"Monthly subscription" (rider pays) and the code's own "riders are salaried" (Deligro pays) describe opposite things. Neither is implemented, so this is a clean-slate decision, not a bug to fix.

4
If riders are salaried: what's the actual monthly figure, and does it vary by rider or route?

Needed to build even the simplest cost-allocation ledger in E.

5
Who is the delivery fee revenue for?

It never reaches the vendor's payout calculation and never reaches a rider ledger — today it simply stays with Deligro by omission. Is that intentional, or is it meant to fund rider pay?

6
What's supposed to happen to a tip on a prepaid (online) order?

The code's own comment says a tip is "paid out in full to the rider," but no code path ever pays anyone. On a COD order a tip is presumably handed over in cash at the door — on a prepaid order it currently has nowhere to go.

7
How should a negative vendor balance (vendor owes Deligro) actually be recovered?

The schema allows it; nothing collects it.

8
Who absorbs the Razorpay gateway fee — Deligro, the vendor, or the customer?

No code deducts it from anyone today.

9
Is GST invoicing/remittance handled by a separate accounting tool, or expected to be built into this system?

Nothing in the codebase generates a tax invoice or tracks what's owed to the government.

10
What's the real settlement cadence — is weekly/monthly actually operated on a schedule today, or ad hoc whenever someone gets to it?

The field exists as a label only; nothing in the system enforces or triggers on it.

11
Should any settlement above a certain amount require a second person's approval before it can be marked paid?

Today one admin account can do the entire cycle alone.

G

Recommended next phase

Ordered by how much money is actually at risk right now, not by build convenience.

Phase 0 — days

Stop the active leaks

  • Fix the two confirmed critical bugs from D: guard every order-completion write against the status it expects, and unwind the delivery row on every cancel path — not just the vendor-reject one. These are live money-out-the-door risks today, not architecture work.
  • Add a refund-clawback correction to the next unpaid settlement whenever a refund is approved against an already-settled order. Currently silent — the fix is small relative to the exposure.
Phase 1 — weeks

Make the numbers trustworthy

  • Add a COD cash-collection confirmation step to the delivery flow.
  • Add a gateway-fee line to the settlement math, even as a configurable percentage, so platform revenue stops being reported gross.
  • Build the minimal financial event log for settlement, refund, and commission-rate-change actions.
  • Decide and implement the commission-timing policy from F.2.
Phase 2 — after the founder answers §F

Build the rider ledger

  • Start with a manual monthly cost-allocation entry per rider — no product change to how riders are actually paid, just visibility.
  • Only after F.3 is answered, decide whether to build a real subscription-to-work model, a per-order model, or keep salary — the shelved riderPayout() function is a usable starting point if per-order pay is chosen.
Phase 3 — before real scale

Reconciliation & controls

  • Automated Razorpay settlement-report import, matched against internal payments.
  • Bank-statement import matched against settlement payment references.
  • Role-based finance permissions and a dual-control threshold on large payouts.
  • A real net-revenue / contribution-margin dashboard, separated from the operational and settlement views per the split in this report.
Phase 4

Compliance layer

  • GST invoicing and tax-remittance tracking, if not already handled by an external accounting tool.
Discovery only — no code, schema, RLS policy, or UI was changed in the course of this audit. Every finding above traces to a specific file, migration, or the prior AUDIT_REPORT_3.md; every number without such a trace is explicitly marked as an assumption.
