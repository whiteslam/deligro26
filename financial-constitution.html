A.1The eleven numbers that must never be confused with each other

TermDefinition
Gross order value (GMV)What the customer pays. Includes money that belongs to the vendor, the tax authority, and the rider. Almost none of it is Deligro's on sight.
Money collected on behalf of vendorThe food value — subtotal, adjusted for who funded any discount. Deligro holds it, doesn't own it, and owes it at settlement.
Platform revenueCommission + GST-on-commission's fee component only — see below — + delivery fee + other per-order charges. This is what Deligro actually earned for running the marketplace.
Delivery revenueThe delivery fee specifically, tracked as its own line inside platform revenue — see E for why it's kept separate.
Rider costThe salaried rider pool, allocated per order for reporting purposes only — it does not change how any individual rider is paid.
Payment gateway costRazorpay's own transaction fee. Deligro's cost, full stop — see B.6.
DiscountsPlatform-funded (Deligro absorbs) or vendor-funded (vendor absorbs). Never customer-funded, never rider-funded.
TaxesA pass-through liability to the tax authority. Sits in a GST/tax-payable ledger, never in Deligro's revenue or profit, even on the portion charged on Deligro's own commission.
Refund exposureMoney Deligro has fronted to a customer that it has not yet recovered from a vendor (or absorbed outright, if the fault was Deligro's or the rider's).
Contribution marginPlatform revenue − gateway cost − rider cost allocation − net refund losses − platform-funded discount cost. The real per-order economics.
Net profitContribution margin − fixed operating costs (staff, infra, rent — outside this system's scope, entered manually into the CFO dashboard).
B

Financial rules

The definitive rule set. Where the audit found existing behaviour marked confirmed, it's preserved here unless a rule below explicitly changes it — changes are called out.

B.1Order pricing rule

Preserved exactly as built: total = subtotal + delivery_fee + tax_amount + tip − discount, floored at zero, computed server-side, discount capped at food subtotal, delivery fee never discountable. No change.

B.2Delivery fee ownership rule

The delivery fee is Deligro revenue, booked as its own "Delivery revenue" line — not silently merged into commission revenue. Internally, it is earmarked as the primary revenue line matched against rider cost in reporting (see G), because the salaried-rider model makes rider cost fixed, not per-order — Deligro should always be able to see whether delivery pricing is actually covering what riders cost.

B.3Tip rule

100% of every tip goes to the rider. Always. The moment a prepaid order with a tip is captured, the tip amount is booked as a rider-payable liability, not revenue — structurally identical to how a customer wallet balance is a liability, not Deligro's money. It is released to the rider on the next payout cycle. On a COD order, the tip is handed over in cash directly at the door and never touches Deligro's books at all — the order record still stores the amount for reporting, but no transfer step is needed.

B.4Commission timing rule — changes current behaviour

Commission is locked at order placement, not at settlement. The rate in effect for that vendor at the moment the order is placed is snapshotted onto the order immediately, the same way price and quantity are already snapshotted onto order items. Settlement then only aggregates already-locked amounts — it stops recomputing anything from whatever rate happens to be live weeks later. Reasoning in full at E.

B.5GST on commission rule

Deligro charges GST on top of its own commission fee to the vendor, exactly as built. That GST amount is not Deligro revenue — it is collected from the vendor and owed to the tax authority, and must sit in the same tax-payable ledger as the customer-facing tax, never inside a profit calculation. decision needed — the statutory correctness of this treatment (and of the customer-side 5% tax) under India's e-commerce-operator GST rules should be confirmed with a CA before scale; this document sets the ledger placement, not the tax law.

B.6Payment gateway fee rule

Deligro absorbs the gateway fee in full. It is never deducted from a vendor's settlement and never passed to the customer as a surcharge — in a single-city, price-sensitive tier-3 market, a payment surcharge would push customers straight back to COD. The fee is recorded as an expense the instant a payment is captured (matching when the gateway actually deducts it), against platform revenue.

B.7Four states of a payment rule

StateMeans
Payment collectedGateway confirms capture. Money has left the customer; payment_status = 'paid'.
Payment settledThe gateway remits that captured amount, net of its own fee, into Deligro's bank account on its own T+N cycle. This is a different event from Deligro settling a vendor payout — the word "settlement" is overloaded in the current system and should be split in naming: "payout" for the vendor side, "bank settlement" for the gateway side.
Platform revenueCommission + delivery fee + other charges, accrued the moment the order's commission is locked — not when cash physically arrives.
Platform profit (contribution)Platform revenue − gateway fee − rider cost allocation − net refund losses − platform-funded discount cost.

D.1Recommendation: Model B, salaried

Three reasons, in order of weight:

1. It's what the product already assumes. Dispatch has no payment gate anywhere in it. The admin settings screen has the per-order rider-commission fields deliberately commented out of the UI. The code's own comments say outright: "riders are salaried monthly, not paid a per-order commission." Building Model A would mean reversing a decision the engineering team already made, not implementing an unfinished one.

2. Rider supply is the harder constraint in a single tier-3 city. A subscription-to-work model works where gig-worker supply is abundant and Deligro can afford some riders declining to renew. In a market the size of Bemetara, a rider-side access fee is more likely to shrink an already-thin rider pool than to generate meaningful recurring revenue — and a shortage of riders directly damages the customer experience Deligro is trying to build a first impression on.

3. Salaried riders give Deligro control over service consistency while the brand is still being established — coverage hours, uniform, response time — the kind of thing much harder to mandate from a rider who is, in the subscription model, effectively an independent contractor buying shelf space.

decision needed This recommendation is not final. Employment classification (salaried employee vs. gig contractor) carries real labour-law and cost-structure consequences that are the founder's call to make, not a system default — see I.1.

E

Vendor settlement formula

The example in the brief — Food Value − Commission − Vendor Charges ± Discount ± Refund — is close, but two terms need to be exact.

Vendor Payable, exact
Food valueOrder subtotal, + platform-funded discount added back (vendor made whole), − vendor-funded discount left subtracted (vendor absorbs its own promotion)
− CommissionFood value × commission %, locked at order time — see B.4
− GST on commissionCommission × GST %, locked alongside it
− Other per-order chargesA flat, configurable operational fee — distinct from the gateway fee, which is never charged to the vendor
− Refund recoveredAny refund on this order already processed before this settlement was built
± Correction adjustmentNew line. A clawback or credit from a refund that landed after a previous settlement for this vendor was already paid — see F, Scenario C
= Vendor payableCan be negative — carried forward automatically into the vendor's next settlement period rather than requiring separate collection, per F

E.1Commission lock point — the decision the brief specifically asked for

Recommendation: order-time snapshot, not settlement-time calculation.

OptionCase for itCase against it
Order-time snapshot recommended Vendor knows their exact net the moment they accept the order — the economics they agreed to fulfil under. Matches standard accrual accounting (revenue recognised when earned, not when paid). Immune to an admin changing the rate later and silently repricing orders already cooked and delivered. Enables real, live per-order contribution reporting instead of waiting for a settlement run. Requires storing a commission field on the order (or a lightweight per-order economics record) rather than deriving it only at settlement — a small, one-time schema addition.
Settlement-time (current) Simpler to build — no extra column, settlement just reads whatever is live. Retroactively changes a vendor's economics for orders already fulfilled if the rate moves between order and settlement date. Breaks the accounting matching principle. No documented policy currently states this is intentional — it appears to be an implementation shortcut, not a considered decision.

This is a policy fix, not an architecture rebuild: snapshot the effective rate onto the order at placement (or vendor acceptance), and let settlement aggregate already-locked amounts exactly as it does today.

F

Refund rules

One invariant governs every scenario below: a refund can never be approved without either removing the order from an unpaid draft, or creating a correction against an already-paid one. There is no third path.

A

Cancelled before any settlement

Customer receivesFull refund
Vendor losesNothing — never invoiced for this order
Deligro absorbsThe gateway's capture fee (usually non-refundable by the gateway itself)
Rider impactNone under the salaried model; a wasted trip is an inherent, tracked-but-not-charged cost
order.cancelled → refund.requested → refund.approved → refund.processed — order never enters a settlement draft
B

Settlement drafted, not yet paid

Customer receivesFull refund
Vendor losesThe order's food value is removed from the still-open draft before approval
Deligro absorbsGateway refund fee, if any
Rider impactNone
settlement.drafted → refund.approved → settlement.draft_invalidated → settlement.rebuilt — a draft may never be marked paid while it contains an order refunded after its build time
C

Settlement already paid

Customer receivesFull refund, fronted immediately — never made to wait on vendor recovery
Vendor losesThe food value they were already paid, recovered via a correction line against their next settlement — not chased in cash unless the balance stays negative long-term
Deligro absorbsThe commission and its GST it already booked on this order, reversed, plus the gateway refund fee
Rider impactNone financially; a post-delivery quality complaint is a service flag, not a pay deduction, under a salaried model
refund.approved → refund.processed → settlement.correction_created (linked to both settlements) → settlement.correction_applied (netted at the next payout)

Customer receivesWallet credit by default — Deligro never held a card/UPI transaction to reverse, and the wallet system already exists in the schema, unused; a manual bank payout only where a customer specifically needs cash out
Vendor losesIf vendor-caused (wrong or spoiled food): the food value they already collected in cash, via a negative settlement line — Deligro effectively invoices them back
Deligro absorbsThe wallet credit, until/unless recovered from the vendor
Rider impactdecision needed — if rider-caused, docking pay is an HR/legal policy call, not a system default; the system should only raise a performance flag automatically
order.delivered → refund.requested → refund.approved → wallet.credited → settlement.correction_created (if vendor-fault)
G

CFO dashboard

Not GMV and commission alone. Four groups, kept visually and structurally separate so nobody mistakes a gross figure for a net one.

Revenue

  • Gross merchandise value (GMV)
  • Platform commission revenue
  • Delivery revenue
  • Other platform fees
  • Rider subscription revenue — shown as "N/A, salaried model" rather than a misleading ₹0, unless Model A is later adopted

Costs

  • Payment gateway fees
  • Rider costs (allocated)
  • Platform-funded discounts
  • Refund losses, net of vendor recovery
  • Other operating costs (manually entered — outside this system)

Profitability

  • Gross revenue
  • Net revenue (gross − refunds − discounts)
  • Contribution margin
  • Contribution margin %
  • Cost per order
  • Profit per order

Cash / settlement

  • Customer money collected (period)
  • Vendor money payable (open)
  • Vendor money paid (period)
  • Pending settlements
  • Refund exposure (open)
  • Unreconciled payments
  • Unreconciled bank transactions

Kept distinct from — but linkable to — the operational metrics (live order/kitchen/rider boards) and accounting-grade settlement ledger detail from the technical audit's admin-requirements section. A founder scanning this dashboard should never need to open a spreadsheet to know whether "revenue" means gross or net.

H.2COD, corrected

The audit's settlement math currently assumes "the shop already holds the cash" for a COD order. Physically, that's usually wrong — the customer hands cash to the rider at the door, not to the vendor at pickup. This document treats the rider as the temporary cash custodian: cash is only considered collected once the rider confirms handover in-app (a required step currently missing entirely), remitted to the vendor or to Deligro on a daily reconciliation cadence decision needed — which path, see I.6, with any shortfall raised as a discrepancy against that rider rather than silently absorbed.

J

Implementation order

Money at risk first. Reporting and polish last — the UI never gets optimised before these rules are settled.

1 — Money currently at risk

Stop the leaks the audit already found

  • The two confirmed critical bugs from the technical audit: cancelled orders resurrected into a payout, and refunds with no settlement clawback. Live exposure today, ahead of any new policy work.
  • Wire the new refund → settlement correction path (F, Scenario C) — this is what actually closes the clawback gap for good.
2 — Financial correctness

Make the rules in this document real

  • Snapshot commission at order time instead of settlement time (B.4 / E.1).
  • Book the delivery fee and tip as their own distinct ledger lines, tip as a liability (B.2, B.3).
  • Add the gateway fee as a recorded expense at capture (B.6).
  • Add the rider cash-custody confirmation step to the delivery flow (H.2).
3 — Reconciliation

Close the loop after money moves

  • Daily rider cash reconciliation (once I.6 is answered).
  • Gateway settlement-report import matched against internal payments.
  • Bank-statement import matched against vendor settlement payment references.
4 — Controls

Remove single points of trust

  • Dual-approval thresholds on refunds and settlement payouts (H, I.7).
  • Structured refund reason codes (I.5).
  • The append-only financial event log itself, with role-based finance permissions.
5 — Reporting

Give the founder the real picture

  • The CFO dashboard in full (G) — revenue, cost, profitability, and cash views kept visually separate.
6 — Optimisation

Only after everything above is settled

  • Rider model refinement (hybrid incentive layers, dynamic delivery-fee pricing, promoted-listing monetisation) — none of it before phases 1–5 are real.
